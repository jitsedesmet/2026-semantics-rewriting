## Related work
{:#related-work}

An imperative rewriting algorithm, such as the one proposed in the
[RDF 1.2 Interoperability note](cite:cites spec:rdf-1-2-interop),
could handle a fixed set of reification schemes. A declarative
approach offers three advantages over such an algorithm: 
<!-- -->
1. extensibility to reification schemes not foreseen at design time,
2. interpretability of the mappings themselves, and
3. reuse of existing SPARQL querying skills.
<!-- --> 
We therefore step back from an imperative algorithm and draw on the
data integration literature, where a Global-As-View (GAV) mapping
describes a global schema as a view over the local sources, and user
queries against the global are [unfolded into queries over the
sources](cite:cites lenzerini2002data).

The closest prior work is [POD-QUERY](cite:cites pod-query), which
applies GAV unfolding to RDF-to-RDF mappings. Each mapping rule names
a single `rml:source` paired with a query over it, expressed in
[RML](cite:cites rml). Because RML rules are scoped to one source,
POD-QUERY inherits a single-source limitation: set-theoretic
combinations across sources $$A, B$$ and $$C$$, such as a global $$D =
(A ∪ B) ∖ C$$, cannot be expressed as a single mapping. We instead
write mappings as SPARQL CONSTRUCT queries, which can federate across
sources through the SERVICE operator and so express such combinations
directly. Expressing mappings in SPARQL rather than RML also lowers
the barrier for RDF consumers, who typically know the former but not
the latter. POD-QUERY additionally predates RDF 1.2 and SPARQL 1.2,
and so does not address the triple-term rewriting that motivates this
work.

A related line of work is [Ontology-Based Data Access
(OBDA)](cite:cites obda, obda-survey), whose reference implementation
is [Ontop](cite:cites ontop). OBDA also relies on GAV unfolding, but
typically maps relational sources -- through mapping languages such as
[R2RML](cite:cites spec:r2rml) -- to an ontology and layers
description-logic reasoning on top. When the sources are already RDF
and full TBox reasoning is not needed, plain GAV mediation is a
lighter-weight fit, and the SPARQL SERVICE operator provides the
federation across sources for free.
